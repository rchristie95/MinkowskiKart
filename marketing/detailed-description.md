# Detailed description

Minkowski Kart is a free, open-source 3D relativistic kart-racing game created by Robson Christie. It begins with the accessible structure of an arcade kart racer, then makes the speed of light a setting that players can bring within reach. As a kart accelerates, the display reports its speed, beta, gamma and accumulated proper time. The result is a racing game in which ideas from special relativity become visible controls, hazards and tactical choices rather than background exposition.

The optical model changes how the course and other moving objects appear. Minkowski Kart uses retarded positions to account for light-travel delay and aberration to alter incoming viewing directions. A conditional Doppler-inspired colour effect can accompany the relativistic presentation. These systems are intended to make high-beta motion legible during a race: players can see the view change while comparing the track clock with the proper time recorded by their own kart.

Relativity also shapes the item set. The time-dilation power-up affects other karts within a limited radius rather than slowing an entire race. A black-hole item behaves as a homing projectile and produces a lensing effect. Wormholes are created as a linked pair: entering one endpoint teleports the kart to the other, with lensing used to signal the effect. They are gameplay representations, not claims of a complete physical model or a true rendered view through a portal.

Minkowski Kart supports keyboard and gamepad controls. Players can race in local split-screen or connect over a LAN. There is no deployed public game server, so online discovery and hosted matchmaking should not be assumed.

The project is experimental. Its driving remains rooted in SuperTuxKart's arcade structure and Bullet-based dynamics, with a configured speed cap. It is not a full special-relativistic vehicle simulation. It does not solve the field equations of general relativity, model a gravitational spacetime around every object or implement full Lorentz contraction. The Doppler colour treatment is conditional rather than a continuous effect applied throughout all high-speed driving. These boundaries are deliberate and important: the game uses selected equations and visual approximations to create an unusual playable experience without presenting itself as a scientifically exact simulator.

Minkowski Kart is derived from [SuperTuxKart](https://github.com/supertuxkart/stk-code) and retains its GPLv3-or-later licensing and attribution requirements. Its mathematical and colour-shift work was inspired by the MIT-licensed [OpenRelativity](https://github.com/MITGameLab/OpenRelativity) project, which is credited in the source. Asset-specific licences shipped with the upstream project also remain part of the distribution.

The latest tagged release is `version_1.9`, dated 18 July 2026. Working experimental builds are supported for Windows, macOS, Linux and Android, and the project owner has confirmed that all four run. Windows CI provides race-entry coverage. Separately, the exact published Windows x64 ZIP matched its expected SHA-256, passed full CRC and extraction checks, contained the executable, `data`, `stk-assets`, `Mobius` and `Minkowski`, and completed a 44.64-second headless race-initialization smoke with no error or crash markers. Its executable is unsigned and root `COPYING` is missing; the publication workflow update adds it to future packages. A Defender command-line heuristic—not a game detection—blocked the hidden graphical-launch attempt, so rendering was not independently retested. macOS has owner-confirmed gameplay plus headless CI. Android has owner-confirmed runtime evidence plus structural CI for ARM64 devices running Android 5 or later. Linux has owner-confirmed runtime evidence, although its archive has a possible missing-`stk-assets` concern for redistributors to check. The iOS package remains an ad-hoc developer build.

Genuine gameplay screenshots are included in the repository; a gameplay video has not yet been recorded.
